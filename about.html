About the project

Fires move fast and are dangerous to approach. This project puts a small, low-cost robot between people and the flames.

Our goal

We are building a low-cost educational prototype of a mobile firefighting robot. It combines robotics, embedded programming, wireless communication, sensors and IoT monitoring.

Checking a possible fire by hand exposes people to heat and smoke, and small robot cars rarely combine flame detection, obstacle response, alarms and remote status. This prototype puts all of these in one system and keeps the operator farther from the test area.

Objectives

  1. Build a robot with a chassis, DC motors, wheels and motor driver.
  2. Detect visible flames with a flame sensor.
  3. Stop the robot for obstacles using an ultrasonic sensor.
  4. Build a five-button handheld controller.
  5. Link remote and robot wirelessly with ESP-NOW.
  6. Show system conditions with red, green and yellow LEDs and a buzzer.
  7. Switch a small water pump through a suitable driver.
  8. Show readings and status on an Adafruit IO dashboard.
  9. Test wireless range, flame detection, obstacle response and pump operation.

How the system is built

PartFunction
Handheld ESP32Reads the five buttons and sends commands.
Robot ESP32Coordinates movement, sensors, alerts and pump.
Flame sensorDetects flame-related infrared light in its field of view.
Ultrasonic sensor (HC-SR04)Measures distance and helps prevent collisions.
Motor driver (L298N)Controls the direction of the DC motors.
Pump driverSwitches the water pump with a properly rated circuit.
LEDs and buzzerIndicate normal operation, fire alerts and obstacles.
Adafruit IOShows selected readings and status when online.

If valid commands are lost, the robot stops its motors. Obstacle-stop takes priority over movement commands.

Expected results

  • Moves forward, backward, left and right, and stops, on command.
  • Communicates over ESP-NOW at a target of at least 10 metres in suitable conditions.
  • Stops when an obstacle is inside the safety distance.
  • Shows normal, fire and obstacle conditions with LEDs and a buzzer.
  • Shows flame, distance, alarm, pump and robot status on Adafruit IO when online.

These are target outcomes, not guaranteed performance. Range, detection and battery runtime will be measured in testing.

Safety and limitations

This is an educational prototype, not a certified firefighting system. It must not replace trained emergency responders, and must never be used to approach a real fire.

  • Never spray water on energized equipment, exposed wiring or electrical fires.
  • Never power the pump directly from an ESP32 pin. Use a rated driver with spike protection.
  • Use matched rechargeable cells, battery protection and a suitable charger.
  • Flame sensors are affected by lighting, flame direction and distance, and do not detect every fire type.
  • Test in small, supervised demonstrations, away from people and flammable materials.
